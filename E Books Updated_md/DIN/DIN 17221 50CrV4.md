Definitions
spring steels according to this Standard are steels which, by reason of their resilience in
quenched and tempered condition, are particularly suitable for the manufacture of spring-
components of all kinds. The resilience of the steels depends on their elastic deformability
enables them to sustain loading within a given range without any permanent set occurring
the load is removed. The properties required of the steels for the purpose of springs are
through higher carbon contents and alloying constituents such as silicon, manganese,
molybdenum and vanadium, as well as through heat-treatment, i.e. hardening in oil or
with subsequent tempering.
For definitions and technical terms relating to heat-treatment, see DIN 17014.
Dimensions and permissible dimension variations
Dimension standards covering the majority of the products mentioned in Section 1.1 are
listed at the end of this Standard.
In the absence of dimension standards for particular forms of product, the permissible
dimension variations are to be agreed when ordering.
Weight calculation and permissible weight variations
The density of the steels covered by this Standard is reckoned as 7.85 kg/dm³.
Unless specified to the contrary in the dimension standards listed, the permissible weight
variations are to be agreed when ordering.
Classification
steel grades
The steel grades listed in Table 1 are divided into quality steels and high-grade steels.
The high-grade steels differ from the quality steels not only in their lower phosphorus
sulphur contents, but also in the uniformity of their properties - particularly in view of
result of heat-treatment - and in the fact that they are largely free from nonmetallic in-
clusions and have a better surface condition.
The selection of steel grade is the purchaser's responsibility. It is advisable that
the manufacturer should be consulted.
treatment condition (condition of delivery)
The steels are generally supplied in the hot rolled condition. They are also available in one
of the other treatment conditions indicated in Table 2, if so ordered.

6. Designations
6.1. Designation of steel grades and treatment conditions
6.1.1. The code numbers have been formed according to Section 2.1.2.2 of the Explanations in Standards Book 3, and the material numbers according to DIN 17007 Sheet 2. To the code number must be appended, as necessary, the code letter, and to the material number the appended number for the treatment condition according to Table 2.
Example for steel 51 Si 7, material number 1.0903 in the soft annealed condition (G or 02): 51 Si 7 G or 1.0903.02.
Appended numbers for identifying the treatment condition (in conjunction with a material number) shall be used only in those cases for which, according to DIN 17007 Sheet 2, issue of September 1961, Section 4.3, there is an unambiguous correlation between the appended number and the treatment condition.
6.1.2. The code number or the material number for the steel grade and the code letter or the appended number for the treatment condition are to be appended to the symbols for the product as given in the examples of designation in the dimension standards.
Example:
Designation of ribbed spring steel of width 80 mm and thickness 10 mm in the steel grade 51 Si 7 (material number 1.0903) in the hot rolled condition:
Spring steel 80 x 10 DIN 1570 - 51 Si 7 U
or Spring steel 80 x 10 DIN 1570 - 1.0903.00
6.2. Designation of modes of delivery
6.2.1. The example quoted in Section 6.1.2 for a code designation is to be written as follows when combined with particulars specifying a mode of delivery (e.g. 3 a, see Table 3):
Spring steel 80 x 10 DIN 1570 - 51 Si 7 U - mode of delivery 3 a
or Spring steel 80 x 10 DIN 1570 - 1.0903.00 - mode of delivery 3 a
