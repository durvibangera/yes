1 Field of application
This Standard defines the technical delivery conditions for seamless precision steel tubes according to DIN 2391 Part 1 which are made from the steel grades listed in section 5.
Tubes according to this Standard are mainly used where accuracy to dimension and, possibly, small wall thicknesses and good surface condition are required.

3 Quality grade
The tubes are supplied in the following quality grades:
A precision steel tubes intended primarily for mechanical stressing, without any particular quality requirement and without acceptance test certificate
B precision steel tubes intended primarily for mechanical stressing, with special quality requirement and exclusively with acceptance test certificate
C precision steel tubes with special requirement according to section 12. These special requirements and corresponding tests must be agreed, with the customer having to specify his requirements at the time of enquiry and on ordering.
4 Designation to be used on ordering
Orders for tubes must specify the following details:
a) quantity
b) reference to this Standard
c) quality grade
d) steel grade
e) condition on delivery
f) outside diameter and wall thickness, possibly also inside diameter and wall thickness or outside diameter and inside diameter
g) length to be delivered
h) certificate on material testing

1000 m tube DIN 2391 – B – St 35 NBK 100 X 3 X 4000 – 3.1 B
If no quality grade is specified, then the tubes are supplied in quality grade A.
If no length to be delivered is quoted, manufacturing lengths in accordance with section 10.3 are supplied.

10.2 Straightness

The permissible deviation from straightness is 0.25 % of the measured length in the case of tubes having outside diameters exceeding 15 mm. In the case of tubes with yield points > 500 N/mm², the deviation from straightness may be up to 0.3 %. This deviation is measured between the tube and a straight line (chord) linking two arbitrary points 1000 mm apart. The maximum deviation from straightness, related to the overall tube length, must not, however, exceed 0.25 % of the tube length or 0.3 % in the case of yield points > 500 N/mm².

In the case of exact lengths under 1000 mm, the maximum deviation from straightness is 0.3 % related to the particular tube length.

In the case of tubes having a ratio of wall thickness to outside diameter of ≤ 1/20, the aforementioned values need not always be complied with.

Tubes with an outside diameter of ≤ 15 mm, are supplied straightened in accordance with normal commercial practice. If this type of tube is also to meet the above requirements, then this must be specially agreed at the time of ordering. Packing must be effected in such a manner that no impairment of the straightness takes place (e.g. packing in crates).

Special requirements in terms of straightness must be mutually agreed.

10.3 Lengths

Where tube lengths are concerned, the following distinctions are made:

a) Manufacturing lengths

between 2 and 7 m. These are the lengths delivered unless at the time of ordering special agreement has been reached concerning the tube length.

The proportion of lengths between 2 and 3 mm does not exceed 15 % of the quantity ordered, and these lengths are supplied in separate bundles.

b) Fixed lengths

with a permissible deviation of ± 500 mm from the length ordered, and underlengths of ≥ 2000 mm may make up at most 10 % of the quantity ordered and are delivered in separate bundles.

c) Exact lengths

Unless otherwise agreed, the following deviations are permissible:

up to 500 mm length: ± 2 mm

over 500 to 2000 mm length: ± 3 mm

over 2000 to 5000 mm length: ± 5 mm

over 5000 to 7000 mm length: ± 10 mm

over 7000 mm length: by agreement

10.4 Tube ends

The tubes must be parted off as accurately as possible perpendicularly to the tube axis. The tubes may be supplied with ends parted off in accordance with conventional part off procedures. This means that at the tube end there may be changes in diameter and also local reductions in wall thickness etc. This does not apply in the case of exact lengths.

If special finishing of the ends is required, this must be arranged by mutual agreement.

**11 Testing**

**11.1 Tubes without acceptance test certificate (quality grade A or C)**

Testing is normally carried out by the manufacturer as part of the continuous quality control, the following tests being involved in this procedure:
a) surface inspection
b) tensile test according to DIN 50 145
c) drift expanding test according to DIN 50 135
d) flattening test according to DIN 50 136
e) dimensional inspection

For tubes of this kind a works certificate according to DIN 50 049 is provided on request.

Drift expanding tests and flattening tests are carried out on tubes made of the steel grades listed in table 2 and in the annealed or normalized condition, only if diameter and wall thickness permit testing.

Tubes of this kind are not normally subjected to leak testing. If the tubes are intended to carry liquids or gases, then appropriate tests must be agreed.

**11.2 Tube with acceptance test certificate (quality grade B or C)**

Tubes according to quality grade B are supplied only with an acceptance test certificate. If tubes according to quality grade C are to be supplied with an acceptance test certificate, then this must be agreed at the time of ordering.

Testing may be carried out by an expert acting as agent of the purchaser. This expert may be an outside inspector or may be selected from personnel of the manufacturer. In accordance with this, an acceptance test certificate 3.1 B or 3.1 C according to DIN 50 049 is made out.

The tests must be carried out at the manufacturer's works in such a fashion that production and delivery of the tubes are not unnecessarily disturbed or held up.

If no random sampling plan has been agreed at the time of ordering, then the random sampling plans employed by the manufacturer apply.

**11.2.1 Extent of testing**

For test purposes, tubes of the same steel grade, the same condition on delivery and the same dimensions are divided into batches of 200 tubes. Residual quantities of less than 20 tubes may be uniformly shared amongst the individual batches. Ordered quantities of less than 200 tubes and residual quantities of between 20 and 200 tubes, count as a full batch.

**11.2.2 Sampling**

**11.2.2.1** One tube is picked at random from each batch and a longitudinal specimen taken for the tensile test.

**11.2.2.2** In the case of annealed and normalized tubes the wall thickness of which is less than 15% of the outside diameter, a specimen for the flattening test is taken from one end of the tube picked up according to section 11.2.2.1.

**11.2.2.3** In the case of annealed and normalized tubes with a maximum outside diameter of 150 mm and a maximum wall thickness of 9 mm, a specimen for the drift expanding test is taken from one end of the tube picked up according to section 11.2.2.1.

**11.2.3 Test methods and results**

All tests must be carried out at room temperature.

**11.2.3.1** The tensile test must be carried out on a measuring length L0 = 5 d according to DIN 50 140. The test result must accord with the values specified in table 4. The yield point is determined only in the case of normalized tubes.

**11.2.3.2 Flattening test**

The flattening test must be carried out according to DIN 50 136. In this test, the specimen or tube ends must be compressed to such an extent that a specific distance H is achieved between the two pressure plates. This distance is defined as follows:

H = (1 + c) * s

where
s is the wall thickness in mm
d is the outside diameter of the tube in mm
c is a constant

The value of c in the case of St 30 and St 35 is 0.09 and in the case of St 45 and St 52 it is 0.07.

In the course of testing, the specimen must not exhibit any unallowable defects attributable to inadequate deformability.

**11.2.3.3 Drift expanding test**

The drift expanding test must be carried out according to DIN 50 135 up to the expansion specified in table 8. In the course of the test the specimen must not exhibit any unallowable defects attributable to inadequate deformability.

**Table 8.**

| Steel grade | Condition on delivery | Expansion in % |
|-------------|-----------------------|----------------|
| St 30 Si    | normalized or annealed | 12             |
| St 30 Al    | normalized or annealed | 7              |
| St 35       | normalized or annealed | 10             |
| St 45       | normalized or annealed | 8              |

**11.2.3.4 Leak test**

If agreed at the time of ordering, the tubes are subjected to a leak test. This test is, at the manufacturer's discretion, carried out by an internal pressure test using water or by a suitable non-destructive testing (e.g. eddy-current testing in accordance with Stahl-Eisen-Prüfblatt (Steel-Iron Test Sheet) 1925 *1).

**11.2.3.5 Visual examination**

Visual examination of the external surface and, to the extent that it is possible, the internal surface too, must be carried out on the tube in a random manner in accordance with a random sampling plan (e.g. according to DIN 40 080).

Visual examination of the tubes must be carried out by the naked eye with suitable illumination.

Instead of visual examination, non-destructive testing of the tube may be carried out using a suitable test procedure, if so agreed.

**11.2.3.6 Dimensional inspection**

The dimensions of the tube must be checked using suitable measuring equipment by a random sampling plan (e.g. in accordance with DIN 40 080).

**11.2.3.7 Check of peak-to-valley height**

The peak-to-valley height of the tube surfaces must be subjected to a random check using a random sampling plan (e.g. according to DIN 40 080) and suitable measuring equipment.

**11.2.3.8 Non-destructive testing of the tubes**

If agreed at the time of ordering, the tubes may be subjected to a non-destructive test method (e.g. eddy-current testing according to PRP 02-74 **1) or ultrasonic testing in accordance with Stahl-Eisen-Prüfblatt (Steel-Iron Test Sheet) 1915 **1).

Explanations

The form of this Standard deviates considerably from the previous July 1967 edition.

Quality grades A, B, and C are new and have been more precisely defined. In this context, tubes of quality grades A or B are no longer distinguished from one another by the steel grade or the condition at delivery but by the tests and the certificates issued in respect of the latter; tubes according to quality grade A are supplied only without an acceptance test certificate and tubes according to quality grade B only with one.

Precision steel tubes are predominantly used as semi-finished products. For this reason, in the example for ordering the statement of the DIN number is followed by the material designation and thereafter the dimensions.

The St 55 steel grade is no longer delivered on any appreciable scale and is consequently not included in this subsequent edition. Instead of St 55, in many applications St 52 is now used, this having a higher yield point and being weldable because of its chemical composition. The soft steel grade St 30 has been nearly adopted.

The reference to the fact that precision steel tubes of quality grade C, may be supplied, e.g., according to DIN 1629, DIN 17 135, DIN 17 175 has been discarded for formal reasons since these are independent tube standards with corresponding delivery conditions.

The conditions at delivery "annealed and descaled" (GZF) and "normalized and descaled" (NZF) are no longer appropriate to the state of the art where precision steel tubes are concerned. They are therefore not incorporated into this subsequent edition.

The limits for tensile strength in the NBK condition at delivery have been determined on the basis of exhaustive statistical analyses. The bottom limits arise from the delayed cooling occurring in continuous furnaces when heat-treating precision steel tubes. An increase in the carbon content in steel grades St 45 and St 52, with the object of raising the minimum tensile strength, is prohibited because weldability is otherwise lost.

Amongst other things, precision steel tubes are characterized by a good surface condition. Consultation and discussion about the "surface condition" section were therefore particularly intensive. Because precision steel tubes are used for many, varied applications, it is not possible to find a definition of surface condition which will satisfy every application. The supply of tubes with special requirements in terms of surface condition must therefore be agreed and the special requirements made known.

The note in the "surface condition" section points to the special problems of defining the concept "crack". Users and manufacturers of precision steel tubes are asked to make their proposals in this context known to the Normenausschuss Rohre, Rohrverbindungen und Rohrleitungen (FR) (Pipes, Pipe Joints and Pipelines Standards Committee) within DIN Deutsches Institut für Normung e. V., Kamekestraße 8, 5000 Köln 1.

Precision steel tubes are used primarily in the form of semi-finished products. For this reason and borrowing from internationally accepted terminology, the term "eccentricity" has been retained for the deviation of centres. The concept of concentricity or coaxiality in accordance with DIN 7184 Part 1 discussed in this context only applies to workpieces and component assemblies, not to semi-finished products.

The stipulation of permissible excess deliveries or shortfall deliveries is a matter of contract law as far as the opinion of some members of the technical committee is concerned. It was therefore proposed that the corresponding section should not be included any longer in these technical delivery conditions. Because the occurrence of excess deliveries of shortfall deliveries, however, is determined by production considerations, this Section has nevertheless been retained.

Because of the high degree of uniformity which is attainable using modern production techniques it is generally possible in the case of precision steel tubes to dispense with full-scale testing of the various properties. Tubes of this kind according to quality grade A are supplied without an acceptance test certificate; if so agreed, the results of tests carried out in the course of production quality control may be submitted in a works certificate.

If it is agreed that an acceptance test certificate should be supplied, then the testing of the mechanical and technological properties (tensile test, flattening test, drill expanding test) must be carried out batchwise. Here, in speaking of tubes of the same condition at delivery, it is intended to indicate that the quality-determining production stages have been performed in the same manner. The association with a specific melting unit, which in the case of other tube standards is used as an order feature, has no relevance to precision steel tubes.

The testing of the other properties, leak tightness, surface condition, dimensions and, possibly, non-destructive testing, is carried out by agreement as either random sample testing or full-scale testing. In this context, depending upon the requirements in relation to further processing or subsequent use, the random sampling plans and the n-c relationships must be agreed. This applies in particular to test procedure and test accuracy in the context of non-destructive testing.

The "complaints" section has been reworded. The technical committee took into account here that it is not always possible or reasonable to reliably prove that a proper processing or application has been impaired as a consequence of defects.

14 Surface protection

Unless otherwise agreed, the tubes are delivered with the temporary corrosion protection normally furnished by the manufacturer.

If a special corrosion protection is required, this must be arranged by agreement. If no corrosion protection is to be applied, this must also be arranged by agreement.

15 Packing

The tubes are normally delivered in bundles secured by steel tape. Otherwise the type of packing must be agreed in accordance with the qualitative requirements.

16 Complaints

The agreed tests (type of test and extent of testing) determine the degree of security against defectiveness.

Complaints about faults may only be considered within the framework of the technical delivery conditions specified in this Standard or other agreements.

The customer must give the supplier the opportunity to judge whether a complaint is justified by submitting the tube objected to and samples of the other tubes delivered.
